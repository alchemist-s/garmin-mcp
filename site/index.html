What it does

Reading the data is the easy part. The point is what Claude can do with it.

Plans from your history

Half or full marathon, 10K, or a rebuild after time off — shaped around the weeks you actually ran, not a template.

Sessions on your watch

Each workout is created as a structured session with pace targets and intervals, scheduled to the day. Change your mind and it reschedules.

Export that actually works

Ask for your running history as a spreadsheet and get one — every run, distance, pace and heart rate. No export queue, no waiting for an email.

Should I run today?

Overnight HRV against your baseline, sleep, Body Battery and recent load — read together, with a straight answer.

Make sense of a block

Why March went well and June didn't. Whether your long runs follow bad sleep. Questions a chart can't answer.

Everything else

Sleep stages, stress, Body Battery, weigh-ins, splits, personal records — all of it available when you ask.

Three steps, once

No Python, no terminal, no configuration files.

  1. Download and open

    Claude Desktop recognises the file and shows an install window.

  2. Sign in to Garmin

    Your email and password. The password is used once, to get an access token that stays on your computer.

  3. Ask for a plan

    If you use two-step verification, Garmin emails a code — paste it into the chat. You won't be asked again for about a year.

Where your data goes

Straight to Garmin

Requests go from your computer to Garmin directly. No server in between, no account to create, no analytics.

Your password is used once

Exchanged for an access token kept on your machine. Never written to disk or logged by Zone Two.

Plans can change; history can't

It creates and reschedules workouts freely. It can never delete a recorded activity — there is no such function in it.

Your questions reach Claude

Whatever you ask about becomes part of your conversation, which Anthropic processes. Worth knowing with health data.

Worth knowing first

A plan from Claude is not a coach. It's a sensible plan built from your own numbers, and it doesn't know about your knee, your work trip or how you actually felt on Tuesday. Treat it as a well-informed starting point you adjust.

It uses Garmin's private app interface. Garmin's official API needs a commercial partner agreement, so Zone Two talks to the same interface the Garmin Connect app does. It isn't supported by Garmin and could stop working if they change it.

What you get depends on your watch. Some models don't compute training readiness or VO2 max. Your activity history is always there, which is what plans are built from.
