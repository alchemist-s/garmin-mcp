Ask Claude about your Garmin data

Sleep, overnight HRV, Body Battery, training load, runs and splits — available in conversation, straight from your own Garmin account.

Download for Claude Desktop View the source

Download the file and open it. Claude Desktop does the rest — no Python, no terminal, no config files.

What you can ask

How did I sleep?

Stages, sleep score, overnight SpO2 and breathing rate.

Am I recovered?

HRV against your baseline, Body Battery, stress, resting heart rate.

How was that run?

Pace, per-kilometre splits, heart rate, power, cadence, elevation.

How's my week been?

Steps, intensity minutes, training load, activities over any date range.

Installing it

  1. Download the file and open it. Claude Desktop shows an install dialog.
  2. Enter your Garmin email and password. The password is used once, to get an access token that is then saved on your computer.
  3. Ask it something. If your Garmin account uses two-step verification, Garmin emails you a code — paste it into the conversation and Claude finishes signing in. You won't be asked again for about a year.

Before you install

It reads; it doesn't delete. Read-only unless you switch on "Allow changes to my Garmin data" at install. Even then it can only rename an activity or log a weight — there is no delete function anywhere in it.

Your data goes to Claude. Anything you ask about becomes part of your conversation, which Anthropic processes. That's how Claude works, and it's worth knowing before you point it at health data.

It uses Garmin's private app interface. Garmin's official API needs a commercial partner agreement, so this talks to the same interface the Garmin Connect app does. It isn't supported by Garmin and could stop working if they change it.

What you get depends on your watch. Garmin only computes training readiness, training status and VO2 max on some devices. On the others those come back empty — that's the watch, not a fault.

Where things run

Everything happens on your own computer. Requests go straight from your machine to Garmin — there is no server in between, nothing is sent to the author, and there is no analytics of any kind. The privacy page spells out exactly what happens to your password and your data, and the source is public so you can check it.
